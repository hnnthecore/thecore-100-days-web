Nettles are the first thing to come up on the combe in spring, and for about three weeks they are exactly right. We pick them in May, before the flowers form, and then we leave them alone.

## Why before flowering

Young nettle tops are soft, green and sweet. Once the plant goes to flower it puts its effort into the stalk and the seed, and the leaves turn coarse and strong-tasting. Picking early also means the plants grow back and give a second, smaller cut in July.

## The morning

We start as soon as the dew is off, around nine, in thick gloves and long sleeves. Each plant is cut a hand's width below the top with sharp snips and dropped into a basket lined with muslin. Nobody is quick. A good picker does about a kilo of fresh tops an hour, which dries down to a little over a hundred grams.

## Straight to the racks

Wilting is the enemy of green tea. The baskets go straight to the barn, where the nettles are spread one layer thick on mesh racks with the doors open and a fan running. They are dry in about two days and still the colour of a spring hedge.

## What it tastes like

If you have only had nettle tea from a bag, the loose leaf is a surprise. It is deeply green, a bit mineral, and slightly sweet, like a spring broth. It is our morning cup, and the one regular customers reorder most.

## A kitchen note

Wear gloves for the picking. Once nettles are dried or cooked, they no longer sting. A handful of fresh tops, blanched for a minute, makes a very good soup.
