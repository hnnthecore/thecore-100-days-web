Drying herbs without an oven

At home, 12 June 2026. 4 minute read.

You do not need any equipment to dry herbs at home. You need a dry room, some string and a bit of attention.

Pick them at the right time

Pick on a dry morning after the dew has gone but before the sun is high, when the plants hold the most scent. Leave the washing: wet herbs go mouldy. If you have to rinse them, shake them well and dry them on a tea towel before you start.

Hang the woody ones

Rosemary, thyme, sage, lavender and mint dry well in small bunches. Tie five or six stems together with string, hang them upside down in a warm, airy, shady spot, and leave them for one to two weeks. A cupboard above the boiler works. A sunny window does not: strong light bleaches the colour and the scent.

Rack the soft ones

Lemon balm, chamomile flowers, lime blossom and leafy herbs like basil dry best in a single layer on a clean tea towel, a muslin-covered tray or a wire cake rack. Turn them once a day. Soft herbs dry in two to four days in a warm room.

How to tell they are done

A dry leaf crumbles when you rub it between your fingers, and a dry flower is papery. If anything bends, it needs longer. If you are not sure, wait another day: damp herbs in a jar will spoil the whole jar.

Storing

Strip the leaves from the stems once they are dry, keep them whole, and put them in a clean glass jar with a lid. Whole leaves hold their flavour far longer than crushed ones, so crumble only what you are about to use. Label the jar with the date. Kept out of the light, dried herbs are at their best for about a year.
