How to brew a proper cup of herbal tea

Brewing, 18 September 2026. 4 minute read.

Most people who tell us herbal tea tastes of hot water have been brewing it from a bag that has sat on a shelf for a year. Loose herbs are a different drink, and the difference is mostly down to three small habits.

Use a little more than you think

A heaped teaspoon of dried leaf or flower per cup is a good start. Whole leaves are light and bulky, so a flat teaspoon of peppermint is a much weaker measure than a flat teaspoon of tea dust. If a cup comes out thin, add more herb before you add more time.

Warm the pot, and cover the cup

Pour a little hot water into your pot or mug first and tip it out. A cold cup knocks several degrees off the water at the worst moment. Then put a saucer or a lid on while the herbs steep. The oils that give mint and chamomile their smell leave on the steam, and a lid keeps them in the cup.

Match the water to the herb

Leaves such as nettle and lemon balm are tough and happy with freshly boiled water. Delicate flowers like chamomile and lime blossom taste sweeter and less bitter if the kettle has stood for a minute. As a rule, a minute off the boil is plenty. You do not need a thermometer.

How long

Four to seven minutes. Peppermint is quick, about four. Nettle and the evening blend like a little longer. Taste at five and decide. When you strain, press the herbs against the strainer with the back of a spoon to get the last of the cup.

A second infusion

Whole leaves and flowers often give a second cup that is gentler but lovely. Leave them in the pot, add more hot water and steep a minute longer.

Iced

Brew double strength, let it cool, pour over ice. Lemon balm and peppermint are the best for this. A slice of cucumber or a sprig of the same herb from a window box makes it look like you tried.
