Journal

A year on the combe

On the farm, 8 January 2026. 5 minute read.

People ask what we do in winter. The honest answer is: plenty, and in a warm barn.

January to March

Orders for seed arrive, and we sow the slow herbs on a heated bench: lavender, echinacea and thyme. We prune the lavender rows in March, once the worst frosts are past. The barn is full of last year’s herbs and the packing table is busy with the tea club.

April and May

The nettles come up first, then the lemon balm and mint. We pick nettle tops in May and have the first cut on the racks before lunch. The chamomile is sown directly into the beds in April, and the comfrey in the wet corner comes back like it was never away.

June and July

This is the long season. Chamomile flowers open in June and have to be picked every other day. Lime blossom needs a week in July when the trees are humming. Lavender is cut when half of each spike is open and hung in bunches. We run the barn fans all day and do not close the doors.

August

A quieter month: the second cut of mint and lemon balm, and a lot of drying and blending. The calendula keeps flowering until the first frost, so we keep picking it. Bees are everywhere.

September and October

Elderberries. The whole family comes for a weekend and we pick from the hedges around the combe until our hands are purple. They are simmered the same day. We lift the echinacea roots in October and start the tinctures.

November and December

Everything slows down. We clear the beds, mulch them with the barn’s old straw and spend the dark afternoons blending the Quiet Hours tea, labelling jars, and writing the Christmas orders. The combe sleeps, and so, a little, do we.
