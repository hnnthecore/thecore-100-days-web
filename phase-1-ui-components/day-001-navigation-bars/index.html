The quiet craft of interface motion

ANAna Novak · 2 October 2026

Good motion is rarely noticed. It answers a question before the user thinks to ask it: where did that panel come from, what changed, and where should I look next?

The best interfaces use easing curves that start fast and land softly, which mirrors how physical objects settle. Linear motion feels mechanical. Expo-out feels considered.

Duration matters as much as curve. Small elements such as toggles, chips and tooltips want 120–200ms. Larger surfaces such as drawers, sheets and modals want 300–450ms. Anything longer starts to feel like waiting.

Then there is restraint. A header that hides while you read and returns the instant you scroll up respects attention. It is present when needed and invisible when not.

Finally, honour the system. When someone has asked their device for reduced motion, every flourish should gracefully step aside. Craft includes knowing when not to animate.

Motion is a material. Like typography or colour, it carries tone, and the tone should be calm, quick and confident.

Scroll back up and watch the header return. That small courtesy is the whole idea.
